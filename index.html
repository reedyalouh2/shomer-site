Shabbat mode

Set it. Then rest.

  1. 3:30 PMFriday afternoon. The ש glows daylight white.
  2. 4:45 PMAs the sun drops, the glow starts to warm.
  3. 5:25 PMDeep amber. Sixteen minutes to candle lighting.
  4. 5:41 PMCandle lighting. The display refreshes and locks.

Shomer knows when Shabbat begins wherever you live. At candle lighting it locks its glow, freezes its display and powers down every sensor. Nothing anyone does at the door changes a single circuit until havdalah.

Sensors off, not ignored.

Touch, motion and proximity sensors lose power entirely. Walking past or touching it changes nothing.

Radio silent.

No app messages, updates or battery alerts. Anything that can wait, waits for havdalah.

Yom Tov aware.

Two-day Yom Tov outside Israel, Yom Kippur, and Shabbat that runs straight into a holiday.

Fails safe.

If Shomer ever loses track of time, it stays in Shabbat mode until it is set again.1

Inside

A sanctuary for the scroll.

The klaf rides in its own sealed capsule, far from the battery and every circuit.

Light-guide ש, e-paper and a logic board built for Shabbat. All behind 1.2 mm of machined aluminum.

    Klaf Passport

    Tap to see who wrote it.

    A secure NFC tag carries your scroll's record: the sofer, when it was written, who certified it, and every inspection since. Printed fake scrolls can't copy it. Shomer reminds you when the klaf is due for a check, twice every seven years.
